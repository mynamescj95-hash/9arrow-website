Reclaiming Your Texas Ranch: When & How to Clear Cedar and Mesquite

If cedar and mesquite have crept across your Texas ranch, you're not just losing views — you're losing water and grazing. Here's how to take your ground back.

Why cedar and mesquite are worth clearing

Ashe juniper (cedar) is a heavy water user, and dense stands crowd out native grasses and shade the ground your cattle need. Mesquite spreads aggressively and chokes pasture. Clearing them reopens grazing, improves water availability, and makes your land more usable and more valuable.

When to clear

The best time is before the problem gets away from you — but it's rarely too late. We mulch cedar and mesquite in place, so there are no burn piles to manage and no waiting on burn bans. In Central Texas, where oak wilt spreads in defined paths, selective clearing is also a smart way to protect the healthy oaks you want to keep.

Selective clearing restores the dream ranch

  • Mulch the brush, keep your best shade trees and live oaks
  • Reopen pasture for grazing and hay
  • Re-establish clean fence lines, sight lines, and access
  • Maintain it with periodic mowing so it never grows back wild
