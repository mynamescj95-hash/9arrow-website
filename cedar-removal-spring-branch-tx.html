How is cedar removal done in Spring Branch, TX?

Cedar removal in Spring Branch, TX is done by forestry mulching the Ashe juniper where it stands. The mulcher grinds each cedar into fine mulch, leaves the root ball in the ground, and works around the live oaks you choose to keep. Nothing is piled, burned or hauled, and the soil stays in place.

Ashe juniper along the Guadalupe River

The cedar in Spring Branch is Ashe juniper, a native tree that grows best on shallow, rocky soils over limestone. Spring Branch sits along US 281 in western Comal County, where the land drops toward the Guadalupe River through steep draws. That is exactly the kind of ground Ashe juniper likes.

Left alone, Ashe juniper closes into dense, nearly solid stands called cedar brakes, especially on canyon slopes. Nearby Guadalupe River State Park has its own Ashe juniper woodland along the river. On private land, a cedar brake shades out grass, crowds the oaks, and makes a property hard to walk, see or use.

Cedar removal around Spring Branch live oaks

Selective cedar removal takes the Ashe juniper and leaves the live oaks standing with open ground beneath them. You decide which trees stay. The mulcher grinds cedar around the oaks and leaves a fine finish under them.

Oak wilt is common in Central Texas. Oak wilt spreads in defined paths, and an infected tree cannot be restored. When oaks on your Spring Branch land are already declining, selective clearing is often the best option to protect the healthy trees around them.

Oak wilt timing for cedar work near oaks

Texas A&M Forest Service advises against pruning or wounding oak trees from February through June. Those are the months when small sap-feeding beetles carry the oak wilt fungus to fresh wounds. Any new wound on an oak can let the disease in, including bark scraped by equipment.

The same guidance says to paint every oak wound right away, in any season. Red oaks are highly susceptible and can die within a month of infection. Live oaks spread the disease underground through connected roots, an average of 75 feet per year. When Spring Branch cedar grows tight against oaks you plan to keep, talk with us about timing before the work is scheduled.

Why cut Ashe juniper stays down and what comes back

Ashe juniper does not sprout from the base once it is cut below its green branches, so a cedar mulched to the ground stays dead. Mesquite and many hardwoods behave differently and resprout from the stump.

New cedar comes from seed. Birds such as American robins and cedar waxwings eat juniper berries and drop the seeds across open ground, and Ashe juniper seedlings can take hold on nearly any site. Catching those seedlings while they are small keeps cleared Spring Branch land open.

Will Ashe juniper grow back after cedar removal in Spring Branch?

Ashe juniper will not regrow from a stump cut below its green branches, so mulched cedar in Spring Branch stays dead. New cedar comes from seed instead. Birds spread juniper berries across open ground, and seedlings can sprout almost anywhere. A follow-up pass every few years catches seedlings while they are still small.

When is the safest time to remove cedar near oaks in Spring Branch?

The safest time to remove cedar near oaks in Spring Branch is outside February through June. Texas A&M Forest Service advises against wounding oaks in those months, when beetles spread the oak wilt fungus. Any oak wound should be painted right away in any season. Cedar work away from oaks is less sensitive to timing.

Is the cedar in Spring Branch really a juniper?

Yes, the cedar in Spring Branch is Ashe juniper, Juniperus ashei, a native juniper that Texans have long called cedar or mountain cedar. Ashe juniper thrives on shallow, rocky limestone soils like the ground around Spring Branch and the Guadalupe River, and it forms dense cedar brakes on slopes when nobody clears it.

What happens to the cedar after removal on my Spring Branch land?

After cedar removal on your Spring Branch land, the cedar is fine mulch spread on the ground where the trees stood. There is no burn pile and nothing to haul. If you want less mulch on the ground, it can be stockpiled or hauled off. A light layer lets grass return within days after rain.
