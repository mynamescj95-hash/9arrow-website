## Forestry mulching / land clearing
- Core of the business. Grinds trees and vegetation where they stand: no pushing, piling, hauling or burning.
- Equipment: compact track machines (tight access, slopes, uneven ground) and large articulated wheel machines (high
  horsepower, high production). The client's own content names a CAT 275 compact track loader and a Weiler M860A mulcher.
- Clears light regrowth and dense brush up to large hardwoods and mature trees. Selective clearing, mass acreage clearing.
- Pace: smaller machines about 0.5 to 1 acre per 8-hour day; larger high-horsepower machines about 1 to 3 acres per day
  with reasonable tree coverage (the service pages say up to 5 acres per day in lighter conditions). Density and terrain
  change the pace.
- No tree size limit; a tree that takes an hour on a smaller machine can take as little as 15 minutes on a large machine.
- Finish: "fine finish", small material described as "toothpicks" or "hamster chips". More appealing, easier to drive over,
  grass regrows faster. Mulching leaves soil intact; dozing disturbs soil and raises erosion risk. Mulch can be stockpiled
  or hauled off if needed.
- Grass regrowth: light mulch, within days after rain; heavy mulch up to 12 inches deep, 2 to 3 years.
- Root balls are left in place with mulching to keep soil stable. When full removal is needed (e.g. under foundations),
  grubbing with bulldozers and excavators; root balls then mulched on site (preferred).
- Brush piles: mulches existing brush piles, including old bulldozer piles "as large as a house", instead of burning,
  which is often prohibited during burn bans.
- Mowing tall grass / light brush with agricultural equipment for lighter needs.
- Tree decline: drought stress and oak wilt are common in Central Texas; oak wilt spreads in defined paths; infected trees
  cannot be restored; selective clearing is often the best option to protect surrounding trees.

## Precision survey line clearing
- Trimble geospatial data collectors and receivers mounted on the forestry mulching machines; GPS guidance in real time.
- Loads the client's licensed surveyor's CAD survey files; follows certified survey data. 9 Arrow is not a licensed
  surveyor and does not replace one.
- Reduces or eliminates survey-crew pre-staking; the client says this can save "tens of thousands of dollars on larger
  projects" and reduces delays.
- Uses: property lines between tracts, land acquisition and pre-development, future road locations through dense timber,
  utility rights-of-way and easements, subdivision street corridors and lot lines, pad sites for oilfield and utility
  projects, fence lines.

## Right-of-way clearing
- For utility companies, service providers and field contractors. Gas pipelines, powerline corridors, waterlines.
- Corridors typically 30 to 120 feet wide, often 90+ feet; routes can run for many miles.
- Often the first contractor on site, opening the easement ahead of grading crews, dozers and excavators.
- Integrates survey data into clearing. Ongoing ROW maintenance with tractor and batwing equipment; manages aggressive
  regrowth such as mesquite.
- Has a written Electrical Right-of-Way (ROW) Safety Manual (page: electrical-right-of-way-row-safety-manual-texas).

## Rock crushing, roads and access preparation
- Processes existing surface rock into a fine, compactible road base on site using crushing heads and attachments that
  break rock down to a fine, consistent grade.
- Benefits (client's list): eliminates imported material, reduces project cost, speeds completion, stronger and more stable
  road surface.
- Road building: land clearing and brush removal, rough grading and leveling, fine grading, road base preparation,
  drainage solutions and erosion control, culvert installation, material processing.
- Roads graded with proper crown and drainage; built to support heavy equipment and truck traffic and stay usable through
  changing weather.

## Site work and light utility installation
- Dozers and excavation equipment; works in rocky terrain and difficult soil; excavation up to about 4 feet deep.
- Reshaping terrain, establishing access where none existed, creek crossings, grading stock tanks, small lakes and water
  features, selective site grading and shaping, cutting pads (including garages), excavating for propane tanks and septic
  systems, trenching for low-pressure waterlines (3 inches or smaller), electrical conduit and septic lines, installing and
  fine-grading pad sites for foundations and future buildings.
- Does NOT: install electrical wire, make final water/electrical tie-ins, excavate swimming pools. Coordinates with
  licensed plumbers and electricians, engineers, general contractors and site supervisors.

## Grounds maintenance
- Routine mowing and weed eating; gas pipeline ROW maintenance, signage clearing, access-corridor upkeep; selective tree
  trimming with chainsaws; hand labor in tight or sensitive areas; property beautification; debris and trash cleanup;
  light demolition of small structures.
- Residential, commercial, rural, ranch headquarters and infrastructure properties, from small acreage to 1,000+ acres.
- Texas cadence (client's guidance): monthly mowing is ideal in season; every 2 to 3 months is the outer limit in peak
  growth; one final mow usually carries a property through winter.
- Recurring agreements available for residential; rural and ranch properties scheduled by season and conditions.
