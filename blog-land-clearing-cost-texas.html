How Much Does Land Clearing Cost in Texas? (2026 Pricing Guide)

If you're planning to clear land in Central Texas, the first question is almost always the same: what's it going to cost? The honest answer is that it depends — but it depends on a handful of factors you can understand and plan around.

What does land clearing cost in Texas?

There's no flat per-acre sticker price for land clearing, because no two tracts are alike. A lightly wooded acre and a cedar-choked acre are completely different jobs. At 9 Arrow, every estimate comes down to one thing: how many hours an area will take to clear. From there we can price the work hourly, daily, by the acre, or as a lump-sum bid — whatever fits your project best.

As a rough planning benchmark, a smaller machine clears about half an acre to an acre per day, while our larger, high-horsepower equipment can clear one to three acres per day under reasonable density. Heavier brush or difficult terrain slows that down; open, lightly wooded ground speeds it up.

The factors that drive your price

  • Tree and brush density — how much material per acre
  • Tree size and species — mature hardwoods take longer than light regrowth
  • Terrain and slope — steep or uneven ground reduces production
  • Access — can equipment reach the site, or does access need to be built first?
  • Soil conditions — sandy soils slow machines compared to hard-packed ground
  • Desired finish — a fine, refined finish takes more time than a rough pass

Why forestry mulching can save you money

Traditional clearing often means cutting, piling, hauling, and burning — multiple steps, multiple machines, and disposal costs. Forestry mulching grinds trees and brush where they stand and returns the material to the soil as mulch. No burn piles, no hauling, no mess. For many Texas properties, that single-pass approach is both faster and cheaper, and it leaves a cleaner, more usable site behind. Learn more about how it works in our guide to forestry mulching.

How 9 Arrow prices a project

We start with a discovery call and, when needed, a site visit. We look at density, size, species, topography, access, and soil, then give you a clear number — hourly, daily, per acre, or lump sum. You know the cost before we start, with no percentage-of-spend games.

“We're not the cheapest. We are the best.” The difference shows up in the finish — and in not having to redo the work later.
